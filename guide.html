  1. 1Lapse rate - how fast the air cools as you climb. Higher = stronger thermals.
  2. 2The call - what that number means (see the scale below).
  3. 3Wind at launch height - direction it comes from and speed in mph, late morning to afternoon.
  4. 4Top of lift - how high thermals should take you, in feet above the valley.
  5. 5Turbulence (B/S) - green is smooth, red is rough (see below).
  6. 6Warnings in red - wrong wind direction, too windy, rain, storms.
  7. 7Models agree - how much the weather models agree. Green = confident, red = unsure.

Turbulence: the B/S number

Compares how strong the thermals are with how much the wind changes as you climb. Strong thermals in light, even wind = high number = smooth. Wind that tears thermals apart = low number = rough.

Coastal soaring sites (Wellfleet)

Wellfleet on Cape Cod is dune soaring, not thermal flying, so it doesn't use the lapse rate. Instead the box shows how many hours, sunrise to sunset, the wind is the right direction (almost due east) and speed:

Soar - full wing6-15 mph for full-size wings.
Soar - mini wing15-20 mph for mini wings and parakites.

It needs at least 2 hours in a row to count. The time under the wind is the best soaring window. Season is November to April; outside that it's shown as Out of season.

Striped boxes and site order

 Striped = not flyable that day (wind or rain). Still shown so you can go check.

Sites are sorted best to worst: sites with a flyable day this week come first, then by lapse rate. #1 is the top pick. A gray number means that site has no flyable day this week.

Small red tags in a box say why a day fails, e.g. Wrong wind, Too windy, Rain. Open the site's details for the full story.

Days 5-7 are shown lighter: forecasts that far out are much less reliable. The numbers are calculated the same way.
